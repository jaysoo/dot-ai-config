## Outcome

The thesis is strong and the draft has a clear arc. The main revision is to present the “background mind” as shorthand for incubation and associative processing rather than a literal second system. The review also identified several absolute claims to soften, one section-order improvement, a missing concrete programming example, unsupported extrapolation from working-memory research, and a closing question that conflicts with the workflow described earlier.

## Follow-up evidence audit

- The cited network-neuroscience review supports interaction between spontaneous, memory-based, and executive processes during creative cognition. It does not study preparation followed by a break and therefore does not support “loading” directly.
- Incubation research supports a narrower sequence: initial preparation, setting a problem aside, and sometimes improved subsequent performance. Results vary by task and break conditions.
- Evidence does not establish that an autonomous background system strategically works on the exact problem it was “fed.”
- No reviewed study directly establishes that continuous AI-agent interaction harms software design or that incubation produces better programming abstractions. That remains the essay’s reasoned extrapolation.
- “Foreground mind” and “background mind” are not standard psychological names for deliberate and incubating thought. Foreground/background terminology is used in perception, attention, and consciousness, but the essay should explicitly introduce its pair as a metaphor for deliberate and spontaneous processing.
- Prefer the established term “preparation” over introducing “loading” as a second name. The foreground/background labels already provide enough original vocabulary.
- Remove the standalone neuroscience caveat about the two modes not being separate systems. The draft already labels them as shorthand, and the caveat interrupts the transition into the directly relevant incubation evidence.
